Frozen pipe emergency: the thaw is when water appears

Ice blocks the pipe and often splits it, but the split stays plugged until the ice melts. That is why homeowners find a frozen line fine at breakfast and flooded by lunch. If you have a frozen pipe that has not split yet, shut the main water valve before you try to thaw it, so any hidden split does not start spraying.

If water is already running, shut the main valve, cut power only if it is dry and safe, and call our 24/7 line. Tell us where the pipe runs, which floor is wet and how long the water has been flowing. After hours, on a holiday or in the middle of a storm, the line is still answered.

Where frozen pipes happen in a Keene house

Pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawl spaces, garages, attics and basement rim joists are the usual victims. The 1971 to 2000 National Weather Service normals for Keene show a cold climate, with about 7,505 heating degree days a year, so a heating failure or a deep cold snap is a real risk here rather than a rare one. Winter vacancy matters too. A second home or a rental left at a low thermostat setting can freeze before anyone notices.

A split pipe in an exterior wall often wets the insulation, which holds water against the framing for weeks. That is why drying is as important as the flood cleanup itself.

How we clean up after a thaw

We begin with extraction and moisture mapping, then open the wall or ceiling only where the readings say water is trapped. Wet fiberglass insulation is usually removed because it does not dry well in place. Dry framing is left alone, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until the readings settle. Our water extraction and structural drying pages describe those steps.

If the split pipe was in the basement ceiling, water may have run onto stored boxes and a finished floor below. We photograph and list what was affected before moving it, so your inventory is in order.

What changes the price of cleanup after a freeze

Frozen-pipe jobs are priced by how far the water spread after the thaw.

  • Time the water ran. A split caught fast wets one wall. One found after a weekend wets several rooms
  • Insulation and cavities. Wet insulation is removed and replaced, which adds labor and materials
  • Cold conditions. Drying in a cold, unheated space takes longer and may need supplemental heat
  • Floors below. Water that reached a lower level widens the drying area
  • Rebuild. Drywall, paint and trim come after drying

Insurance and frozen pipe water damage

Many policies cover sudden freeze damage when the home was kept heated, though terms differ and some require reasonable steps to prevent freezing. Read your policy or ask your insurer. We make no coverage promises, but our readings and photos show what the freeze actually did.

Frozen Pipe Water Damage questions, answered

Can I call during a deep freeze at night?

Yes, and cold snaps are exactly when we expect calls. The line is staffed around the clock. We will not quote an arrival time over the phone, but we will explain what to do about the frozen section while the crew is arranged.

Should I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

You can try gentle warmth with a hair dryer or warm towels, but close the main valve first. Never use an open flame. If you cannot find where the pipe runs, call a plumber.

Will my walls need to be opened after a pipe splits?

Not always. Moisture readings tell us whether water is trapped. Often a small opening or two lets us dry the cavity. Wet insulation is the common reason walls do get opened.

How long does drying take after a freeze flood?

Usually several days, depending on materials and temperature. Cold spaces dry slower, so we may bring in extra heat. We check readings daily and stop when they come back to a normal range.
