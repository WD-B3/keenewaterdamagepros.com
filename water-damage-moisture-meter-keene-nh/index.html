How a Moisture Meter Finds Water Damage in Keene, NH

A moisture meter does not say whether a wall is damaged. It says how much moisture is in the material compared with a dry reference. That comparison is how crews decide what to dry, what to cut out and when the job is done.

What a moisture meter measures

Pin meters push two small probes into wood or drywall and measure electrical resistance. More moisture lowers resistance, so the reading rises. Pinless meters send a signal through the surface and measure how it changes, which lets a tech scan a wall without leaving holes. Both give a number on a scale that differs by material.

That last point matters. A reading of 18 on a wood scale is a different thing from an 18 on a drywall scale. A tech who reads the number without checking the material setting can get a wrong answer.

Why a dry reference matters

There is no single number that means dry for every house. The standard approach, described in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, is to measure unaffected materials in the same building and use those as the drying goal. A drywall wall in a basement that never got wet may read higher in August than one in a heated living room in January.

So the first reading a tech takes is often of a dry wall in a part of the house that was not touched. Everything wet is dried until it matches that.

How crews map a wet room

A tech starts at the visible stain and works outward until readings fall to the dry reference. The edge is marked on the wall and photographed. Then the tech checks the floor, the baseboard, the cabinet toe kicks and the ceiling below if there is one. Water travels, so the biggest wet area is often not where the stain is.

That map decides what happens next. Clean drywall with light moisture may dry in place under air movers. Soft, swollen or stained drywall is removed. Wet insulation is replaced. The details are on our wall and drywall page.

When can the equipment come out

Drying is done when the wet materials match the dry reference, and the readings hold for a day without the equipment. Crews log readings every day so the trend is visible. If the numbers stall, something is still feeding the moisture, such as a damp slab, a leaking pipe or a high-humidity crawl space. We then keep looking, not keep running the fans.

Readings that mislead

Meters can be fooled. Metal studs, nails, foil-backed insulation and pipes inside a wall can push a pinless reading up. A fresh coat of paint or wallpaper can mask the real number, and salts left behind by old leaks can make a dry wall read wet. A thermal camera can be misled by a cool exterior wall that has never been wet.

Good techs cross-check. They read the same spot with two tools, compare it with a dry wall nearby and take a second reading the next day. One high number is a question, and a trend over several days is an answer.

Good habits for reliable readings

  • Check the meter on a piece of wood or drywall you know is dry.
  • Set the right material scale before you read.
  • Read a grid, not a single spot, and mark each spot with tape.
  • Write down the number, the place and the time.
  • Read again the next day to see which way the numbers move.

Consistent method matters more than the brand of meter.

Why Keene houses give odd readings

Older homes near downtown Keene mix plaster and lath, wood framing and brick or stone foundations. Plaster can read very differently from modern drywall, and a stone foundation can hold moisture for a long time after the water is gone.

That is one reason we prefer to compare readings against dry materials in the same house. A basement in Swanzey or a farmhouse crawl space in Westmoreland has its own baseline.

Can I use a meter myself?

A consumer meter can tell you whether one spot reads higher than another, which is useful for a quick check. It will not map a wall or tell you if the floor under a laminate is wet. If you are weighing a purchase of a meter, treat it as a screening tool. Anything that points to a real leak deserves a professional check. See our water extraction page for the next steps.

More on moisture meters explained

What reading means my wall is dry?

There is no universal number. Crews compare wet areas against unaffected materials of the same type in the same building.

Can a meter find a leak inside a wall?

It can show where a wall is wet, which points toward the leak. A thermal camera helps confirm it. A plumber finds and fixes the leak.

Do I need a thermal camera if I have a meter?

A camera finds wet areas faster over a wide space, then the meter confirms. Used together they miss less.

How often are readings taken during drying?

Typically once a day until the materials match the dry reference.
