Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage in Keene, NH

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete floor. The leak is hidden, so the damage shows as floor moisture, not as a drip. We handle the water damage once a plumber finds and stops it.

Warm spot on the floor or a water bill that jumped?

Those are the two signs people notice first. A line under a concrete slab can leak for weeks, wetting the floor from below, warming a patch of tile or carpet and quietly raising the bill. A hidden leak under a concrete floor is called a slab leak, and it needs a plumber with leak-detection equipment before anyone cuts anything open.

Slab leak emergency: what to do right away

If water is visible on the floor, shut the main valve and call a plumber. Listen for running water when every tap is off and check the water meter. If the dial moves with everything closed, a line is leaking somewhere. If the slab is wet near walls or baseboards, call our 24/7 line so we can start extraction and moisture mapping.

The general cleanup steps match our water extraction page, with one twist. The plumber has to find the leak first, because we cannot dry a floor that keeps filling from below.

How common are slab leaks in Keene homes?

We cannot give a local figure, and we will not invent one. Many New Hampshire homes sit on basements or crawl spaces rather than slabs, so a slab leak is more likely in additions, garages, some newer construction and some commercial buildings that have slab-on-grade floors. If your home has a concrete floor on grade, the page applies to you. If it has a basement, see our basement water damage cleanup page instead.

To know what your house has, call the city's building office about the slab or check your own records. If you are unsure whether a leak is under a slab or in a wall, a plumber with detection tools can tell you.

How we handle the damage

Once the plumber has located and stopped the leak, we remove standing water, check how far moisture traveled through flooring, baseboards and lower walls, and dry the area. Floors on a slab can be wet from below, which makes drying slower for hardwood and laminate. Those materials may have to come up. Concrete itself holds water and dries slowly, so we use dehumidifiers rather than only air movers.

If tile or carpet is lifted, we document the subfloor and the slab surface before replacement. You may need new flooring, but we will tell you whether yours can be dried and saved. See hardwood floor water damage repair and carpet water damage restoration for how those floors are handled.

What affects the cost

A slab leak has two costs, the plumbing repair and the water damage cleanup, and only the second is ours.

  • How long the leak ran. A slab that stayed wet for weeks needs longer drying
  • Flooring type. Tile dries differently from hardwood or carpet
  • Area affected. More wet rooms mean more equipment
  • Concrete drying. Slabs hold water and need dehumidifiers for several days
  • Access. Cutting back baseboards and lower drywall adds labor

Insurance and slab leak water damage

Policies treat slab leaks in different ways. Some cover resulting water damage but not the cost of locating and repairing the pipe itself. Read yours or ask your insurer. The final word on coverage is theirs, and we provide the records.

Slab Leak Water Damage questions, answered

Water is coming up through the floor late at night. Now what?

Shut the main valve, call a plumber and call us. The line is open 24 hours a day. We respond the same day when we can, with no promised arrival time, and we handle the water damage once the line is found.

Why is part of my floor warm and my water bill higher?

A warm patch and a rising water bill together can point to a hot water line leaking under a slab. A plumber with leak detection tools should confirm it before anything is opened. We handle the water damage that follows.

Can you fix the pipe under my slab?

No. A plumber locates and repairs or reroutes the line. We remove the water and dry the structure.

Will my concrete floor have to be removed?

Not always. The plumber decides whether the line is repaired through the slab or rerouted around it. We handle the water that came out.
