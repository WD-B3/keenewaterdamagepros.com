Commercial water emergency: first steps

Shut off the water at the main or at the failed fixture, and cut power to wet areas only if it is safe. Move inventory, electronics and records off the floor. For a business, tell your building manager or landlord as well as your insurer, because lease terms and who maintains which pipes affect the claim.

Call our 24/7 line next. Give us the size of the space, what is wet and what must stay operating, such as a refrigeration unit, server closet or a shop floor. We cannot promise a specific arrival time, but we will plan the response around your priorities.

How commercial losses differ from home losses

A house has one occupant group and one insurance claim. A commercial building may have tenants, a landlord, a property manager and several policies. Floors may be commercial carpet tile, sealed concrete or vinyl, and ceilings are often suspended tile that soaks through quickly. Drying has to avoid disrupting neighbors, and sometimes work happens at night.

Downtime changes the plan. We stage work so one section of a space can reopen while another dries, where the layout allows. We document everything because business interruption claims depend on records. For the drying process itself, see structural drying, and for contaminated water see sewage backup cleanup.

Keene commercial buildings: older stock and mixed use

Downtown Keene around Central Square mixes storefronts, offices and apartments, often in older buildings, and the city also has former mill buildings such as the Beaver Mills complex on Railroad Street, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Older masonry and wood-frame buildings with multiple tenants mean a leak on an upper floor can travel through shared walls and ceilings to a unit below.

That is why we ask who is above and below when we assess a loss. A storefront with an apartment overhead needs a plan for the ceiling and the unit above. Historic buildings may also have rules about alterations, so check with the city's building office before you remove or rebuild anything structural.

What affects commercial pricing

Commercial jobs vary widely, so we scope each one and explain it before work begins.

  • Square footage. Big floor plates mean more equipment and more labor hours
  • Materials. Carpet tile, drywall, ceiling tile and sealed concrete dry differently
  • Hours and access. Night and weekend work or tenant coordination adds labor
  • Contamination. Gray or black water needs protective gear and disposal
  • Documentation. Detailed logs and photos for insurance and landlord records

Insurance and commercial water damage

Commercial policies differ a great deal, and business interruption coverage is separate from property coverage. Read your policy and talk to your agent early. Approval is not something we can offer, but our logs and photos support the claim.

Commercial Water Damage questions, answered

Can a business get help after closing time?

Yes. The dispatch line is open every hour of every day, which matters when a pipe lets go after the doors lock. Same-day response is the aim, and arrival times are not promised.

Can you work around our business hours?

Often, yes. We stage work by area and schedule noisy steps for off hours when possible. Tell us what has to stay open and we will plan around it.

Do you work with landlords and property managers?

Yes. We document the loss so the landlord, tenant and insurers each have what they need. Who pays for what depends on your lease and policies.

What about water that traveled between tenant spaces?

We check units above and below with moisture readings, because water follows shared walls and ceilings. We record each space so claims can be separated.
