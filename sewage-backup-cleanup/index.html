Raw sewage coming up a drain: what to do first

If water is rising from a floor drain, toilet or tub and it is dark or smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated. Water like this is classed as Category 3, or black water, in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and it should not be touched without protection.

Sewage backup emergency: safety steps before we arrive

Stop using water in the house. Do not flush, run the washer or take a shower, because every drain feeds the same line. Keep people and pets out of the wet area. If you can do it safely, call your sewer or plumbing contractor to find the blockage, and call our 24/7 line for the contaminated water.

If the water has reached outlets, a panel or a furnace, stay out and report the electrical hazard to the utility or 911 before you call us. Do not try to clean it yourself with a mop or a shop vacuum, because that spreads contaminants and can aerosolize them.

How we clean up after sewage

Crews wear protective gear, remove the contaminated water and take out porous materials that cannot be reliably cleaned, such as carpet, pad, insulation and some drywall. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, and air movers and dehumidifiers finish the dry-down. We bag and dispose of contaminated materials properly rather than leaving them for you to carry out.

We also tell you honestly what cannot be saved. Upholstered furniture and soft goods that sat in sewage water are usually discarded. We list what was removed and photograph conditions, which helps your insurance conversation. For the drying phase, see structural drying.

Where sewage backups happen in a Keene house

Backups usually show at the lowest drain in the house, such as a basement floor drain, shower or toilet. They come from a blockage in the home's own line, from tree roots or a collapsed section, or from a problem in the municipal main. The City of Keene Public Works Department runs the city sewer, so if neighbors are also backing up, ask Public Works whether the main is the issue.

After heavy rain or snowmelt, municipal lines can surcharge and push water into basements. That matters for insurance because sewer backup coverage is often an add-on to a homeowners policy. Check your policy for it. If water entered from outside, our flood damage cleanup page applies.

What affects the cost

Contaminated water has a safety and disposal component that clean water does not.

  • Category 3 handling. Protective gear, sanitizing and disposal raise labor and supplies
  • Area and depth. A single bathroom costs less than a whole basement
  • Porous materials. Carpet, pad and wallboard touched by sewage are discarded, not cleaned
  • Drying. After sanitizing, dry-down time follows the readings in each room
  • Rebuild. Replacement of flooring and walls is scoped after cleaning

Insurance and sewage backup cleanup

Sewer and drain backup coverage is often sold as an endorsement, not included in a standard homeowners policy, so do not assume it is there. Read yours or ask your insurer. Whether a claim is paid is not our call, and we document the loss carefully.

Sewage Backup Cleanup questions, answered

Sewage is backing up overnight. Do you respond?

Yes. Stop using water, keep everyone out of the affected area and call. The line is open around the clock. Same-day response is the aim, and an exact arrival time is never promised.

Can I clean up a small sewage backup myself?

We do not recommend it. Even small backups carry contaminants, and mopping or vacuuming can spread them. Protective gear and proper disposal matter.

Why is my toilet or floor drain backing up?

A blocked line in your house or in the municipal main is the usual reason. A plumber finds the blockage. If neighbors have the same problem, tell Keene Public Works.

What gets thrown out after sewage damage?

Porous materials such as carpet, pad, insulation and some drywall are removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned. Soft furnishings that sat in sewage water are usually discarded.
