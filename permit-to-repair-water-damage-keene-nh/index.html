Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Keene, NH?

Drying a house usually does not need a permit, but rebuilding can. The City of Keene says cosmetic work often does not and structural, electrical and plumbing work does. Here is how to sort your project.

Drying, demolition and rebuilding are different jobs

A water damage project has stages. Emergency work stops the water and dries the structure. Demolition removes what cannot be saved. Rebuilding replaces drywall, insulation, flooring and sometimes framing, wiring or plumbing. Permit questions mostly sit with the last stage, though demolition of structural parts may raise them too.

What the City of Keene says

The City's Community Development Department states on its FAQ page that many cosmetic improvements or renovations do not require a permit, but structural, electrical and plumbing work do. The page points to Section 44-5 of the City's ordinance for items that are exempt, and it advises residents to call the office if they are unsure.

Applications go through the City's online permit portal, and the office is on the fourth floor of City Hall at 3 Washington Street. We are not the permit authority and we do not decide what you need. Ask the department about your specific project.

How to ask the city a useful question

  • Describe the work in plain terms, such as replacing wet drywall and insulation in one room.
  • Say whether framing, wiring or plumbing will be touched.
  • Ask whether the work needs a permit, who has to apply and what inspections follow.
  • Ask whether a licensed trade is required for any part.
  • Write down the answer and the date.

Your insurer may also ask for permit records or contractor invoices. Keep copies.

Who pulls the permit

That depends on who does the work. A licensed plumber or electrician usually handles permits for their own trade. A general contractor handles it for a rebuild. A homeowner doing work on their own home may be allowed to apply, but the city sets the rules. We handle water removal and drying and do not rebuild. Many homeowners hire a contractor for repairs once the structure is dry. See our wall and drywall page and ceiling repair page.

What to bring when you call or visit

  • The address and a short description of the work.
  • Photos of the damage and the areas to be opened.
  • A drawing or list of rooms involved, even a rough sketch.
  • Contractor names and license information if you have hired anyone.
  • Your insurer's scope or estimate, if you have it.

Showing up with this information saves time and gets a clearer answer. Ask what inspections are required and how to schedule them.

Insurance and permits

Insurers sometimes ask for permit records on larger rebuilds, and an unpermitted repair can complicate a later claim or a sale. If your project is large, ask your adjuster whether permit costs are part of the estimate. Keep the final inspection record with your other house papers.

A sample project, step by step

  1. A supply line behind a bathroom wall fails and the wall gets wet.
  2. A crew extracts water, removes wet drywall and dries the cavity.
  3. A plumber replaces the failed line, and asks the city about a plumbing permit.
  4. A contractor closes the wall, and asks about any inspection needed before it is covered.
  5. Paint and trim go on last.

In this sequence, drying and demolition happen first and the permit questions come at the repair stage. The order can differ, so ask each trade what they need and when.

Fees, timing and delays

Ask the office what the fee is, how long review takes and what inspections the permit requires. If an inspector has to see the work before walls are closed, plan the schedule so that drywall does not go up first. Delays are far less costly when they are planned for, and the drying work can proceed in the meantime.

Outside Keene, different rules

Permit authority follows the town line. A repair in Swanzey or Marlborough falls under that town's rules, not Keene's. Many small towns handle building permits through a selectman's office or a part-time inspector.

Call the town office where the house sits. We do not assume the answer is the same.

Do not let permits delay drying

Drying and mitigation are urgent. Wet materials grow mold in a day or two, and waiting for permit paperwork on the rebuild should not delay extraction and drying. If an emergency repair, such as shutting off a broken line, is needed, do that first and ask the city after. See our structural drying page for the timeline.

More on permits for water damage repairs

Do I need a permit to dry out my house?

Drying and extraction are generally not construction, but ask the city if you are unsure. Rebuilding structural, electrical or plumbing parts usually involves permits.

Does the City of Keene say replacing drywall needs a permit?

The City's FAQ does not address drywall specifically. It says cosmetic work often does not need one and structural, electrical and plumbing work does. Call the office.

Can a restoration company pull permits for the rebuild?

We handle water removal and drying. Rebuild permits are usually pulled by the contractor who does the work.

What if I skip the permit?

It can cause trouble with inspections, insurance and resale. Ask the city before you start.
