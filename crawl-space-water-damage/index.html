A crawl space is out of sight, so water there can sit for weeks. We remove standing water, dry the joists and subfloor from below, and flag damp insulation and mold risk before they affect the rooms above.

Signs of water in a crawl space

A crawl space speaks through the rooms above it. Musty air in the first floor, floors that feel soft or bouncy near an exterior wall, a cold floor in winter and mold-looking spots on joists are all signs. If you have a hatch, a flashlight shows standing water, wet insulation hanging from joists or a sheet of plastic that has slid off the ground.

Do not enter a crawl space with standing water if you cannot see wiring, ducts or pipes that may be low. Electrical hazards and tight space make it risky. Call us and describe what you can see from the hatch. For an active flood, our line is open all hours.

Why crawl spaces take on water

Crawl spaces fill from three directions. Groundwater and snowmelt seep in through the soil or foundation walls, plumbing leaks drip from above and humid summer air condenses on cool surfaces. Annual precipitation near 41 inches, per federal normals, means a thaw after a snowy winter is a common time for water to appear.

How your crawl space was built changes the plan. Some have a plastic vapor barrier, some have a dirt floor and some have a poured floor, so tell the dispatcher what you know. Restoration deals with the water and the damage. Waterproofing and grading are different trades, and we will tell you honestly if what you need is a waterproofing contractor rather than a restoration crew.

How we handle a wet crawl space

We pump standing water, remove wet insulation that has fallen or soaked through and dry the joists and subfloor with air movers and dehumidifiers set up through the hatch. Because the space is closed, it can take longer to dry than a room, and humidity stays high until air is moved. We check wood moisture before and after, and we photograph what we found.

If we see mold on framing or the underside of the floor, we tell you plainly and explain whether it needs containment and cleaning under the S520 mold standard. Our mold remediation page covers that path, and our structural drying page explains the drying process.

What affects the cost

Price depends on the space and what the water touched.

  • Access. A tight hatch and low clearance slow every step
  • Standing water. Depth and area set pump time
  • Insulation. Wet insulation is removed and may be replaced
  • Framing moisture. Wet joists need longer drying
  • Mold on framing. Growth on joists or the subfloor adds containment and cleaning to the scope

Insurance and crawl space water damage

Groundwater seepage and flooding are usually excluded from standard homeowners policies, while a sudden pipe failure may be covered. Ask your insurer which one applies to you. We give you the readings and photos, and coverage stays between you and your insurer.

Crawl Space Water Damage questions, answered

Can you work on a flooded crawl space at night?

Yes, for active water. The dispatch line is open around the clock. Tight access can slow a job, so we ask about the opening and the water depth on the call. No arrival time is promised.

Is it safe to go into a flooded crawl space?

Not if wiring, ducts or pipes are low or you cannot see clearly. Stay out and describe what you see from the hatch. We will tell you what is safe.

Does my crawl space need waterproofing?

Possibly, if water keeps coming back. Waterproofing and drainage are different from restoration. We dry and repair the damage, and we will say so if the cause needs another contractor.

Will musty smells go away after drying?

Often, if the moisture source is gone and the area dries fully. If mold or damp insulation remains, the smell can stay until it is cleaned or replaced.
