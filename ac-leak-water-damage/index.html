AC Leak Water Damage in Keene, NH

Air conditioners and heat pumps remove moisture from the air, and that water has to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs or the pan overflows, the water goes into ceilings, closets and walls. We dry what got wet and flag mold risk.

AC leak signs and what to do first

Turn the system off at the thermostat the moment you see water. Air handlers keep producing condensation while they run, and a blocked drain line turns that into a steady drip. Put a bucket or towels under the area, then call a heating and cooling technician to clear the line and check the pan. We handle the water that has already left the system.

Common signs are a ceiling stain near an air handler in the attic or a closet, water around a wall-mounted unit, a musty smell near vents and a float switch that keeps shutting the system off. Our 24/7 line is open if the water is active, and during business hours for a stain that has been there a while.

Where condensate water ends up

A central system's evaporator coil sits in a cabinet that collects condensation in a pan, and a drain line carries it outside or to a drain. When algae and debris clog the line, water backs up, fills the pan and spills over. In an attic or above a finished ceiling, the water ends up in insulation and drywall. In a closet or utility room, it soaks the floor and the bottom of the wall.

Mini-split and wall units can leak at the indoor head too, usually down the wall and into baseboards. In each case the water is usually clean condensate, but it can hold algae, and when it sits in insulation or drywall it grows mold.

How we assess and dry an AC leak

We map moisture around the air handler with a meter and thermal camera, trace the wet path across ceiling, wall and floor, then remove material that cannot dry, such as soaked insulation or soft drywall. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the rest. Because the water is slow and persistent rather than sudden, we also look for mold, especially in closets, attic spaces and on the back of drywall.

Preventing the next one

  • Have the drain line cleared by an HVAC technician before the cooling season starts
  • Ask about a secondary drain pan and a float switch that shuts the system off
  • Check the attic or closet around the air handler a few times each summer
  • Do not ignore a musty smell near a vent. It may mean water is sitting somewhere
  • Keep a bucket and towels near the unit if it has leaked before

What affects the cost

AC leaks usually start small, and cost follows how far the water went.

  • How long it leaked. Weeks of drip wet more insulation and drywall than a single overflow
  • Where the unit sits. Attic installs add access labor and ceiling damage below
  • Materials removed. Wet insulation and soft drywall are removed and replaced
  • Mold check. Dark growth near the drain pan can add containment and cleaning
  • Drying days. Ceiling cavities take longer to dry than open rooms

Insurance and ac leak water damage

Slow condensate leaks that went unnoticed for months may be treated differently from sudden failures, and policies differ on what they cover. Ask your insurer how yours reads. We cannot speak for it, but we can show what was wet and for how long.

AC Leak Water Damage questions, answered

Do you answer calls about an AC leak on a weekend?

Yes, if water is spreading. Switch the system off at the thermostat, then call. Someone answers at every hour, we work toward a same-day visit and we never name a time.

Is AC condensate dirty water?

It is usually clean water, but it can carry algae and dust, and it grows mold if it sits. Treat it as a mold risk if it stayed wet for more than a day or two.

Do you fix the air conditioner itself?

No. An HVAC technician clears the drain line and repairs the unit. We dry the building and handle the damage.

Can I keep running the AC while you dry the area?

Ask your HVAC technician first. If the drain is still clogged, running it will keep making water. We will coordinate with your plan.
