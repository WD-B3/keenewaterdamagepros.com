How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Keene, NH

You may be choosing in the middle of an emergency, which is when pressure sales work best. A short list of questions will tell you whether a company dries structures properly or just runs fans.

Start with what the job should include

A proper water damage job has a clear sequence. The company finds and maps the wet area with meters and a thermal camera, removes standing water, sets drying equipment sized to the room, takes moisture readings daily, and removes what cannot be dried. Then it confirms dryness with readings and gives you a written record. If a company skips the readings, you cannot tell when the job is finished.

Questions to ask on the phone

AskA good answer sounds like
Do your crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard?Yes, and they can explain how they decide a structure is dry
Are you licensed and insured?A clear yes, with proof available on request
What equipment will you bring?Extraction units, air movers, dehumidifiers, moisture meters, a thermal camera
Will I get readings and photos?Yes, daily readings and photos before and after
How will you price the job?A scope and price explained before work starts
Can you work with my insurer?They provide documentation. They do not promise approval

Listen for specifics. A vague answer to the equipment question or the readings question is a reason to keep looking.

Red flags

  • A promise that your insurance will approve the claim. No company controls that.
  • A contract that asks you to sign over insurance benefits before work starts.
  • A flat price quoted without seeing the loss.
  • Pressure to decide in minutes, or to sign before you read.
  • No moisture meter and no drying log.
  • Tearing out far more than the readings support, or leaving wet material in place.

Standards, licensing and certification

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes accepted practice for water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. Ask any company whether its crews follow them. Rules for contractors vary, so ask what licenses apply to the work being done, and ask for proof of insurance. We use generic wording on our site on purpose. We dispatch to licensed and insured restoration professionals and do not list individual credentials.

Asking is your right. A company that answers readily is a better bet than one that changes the subject.

Pricing and scope

Water damage prices depend on the water category, the area, the materials and the days of drying. A trustworthy company explains the scope and price before work starts, and tells you when the scope changes. Be wary of an estimate by phone with no inspection. Our water extraction and structural drying pages list the factors.

What a good first visit looks like

The crew checks for safety hazards, finds the water source, takes moisture readings and photos, and explains the plan in plain terms. It should tell you what will be extracted, what will be removed, what equipment will run and for how long. It should also tell you what it does not know yet, because some answers come after the walls are opened.

Reading the estimate

A useful estimate lists the areas, the work, the equipment and the days. It states what is included in the price and what would change it. If you see a lump sum with no detail, ask for a breakdown. If a figure seems low, ask whether it includes monitoring visits and final readings. Keep the estimate with your claim file.

Contract terms to read before you sign

  • The scope of work, in writing, with the areas and tasks listed.
  • How price changes are handled if the scope grows.
  • Any request to assign your insurance benefits to the company, and what that means.
  • When payment is due and what happens if the insurer pays less than the invoice.
  • Who is responsible for permits, disposal and cleanup.

Take your time reading, even in an emergency. A reputable company will let you read the paper first and will explain anything you do not understand.

Speed versus quality

Fast response is important, since water damage grows by the hour. But fast does not mean rushed. A good crew can start extraction quickly and still take time to map the wet area and explain the plan. Be wary of a company that rushes you to sign before it has looked at the damage. Speed that skips readings usually costs more later.

Local matters more than it sounds

A company based near you knows the houses. In the Keene area that means stone foundations, old plumbing, plaster walls and long runs to rural towns like Westmoreland and Walpole. It also means a crew that can reach you without a long drive.

Ask where the crew will come from, and ask for an honest description of timing. Nobody can promise an exact arrival time, and you should be skeptical of a company that does.

After the job

Ask for the drying log, the final readings, the photos and the invoice. If any materials were removed, ask for a list. Keep them with your insurance claim file. If you see a musty smell or stain return, call and ask for a re-check.

More on choosing a restoration company

Should I pick the company my insurer recommends?

You can, but you are usually free to choose. Ask your insurer about requirements and compare scope and records.

Is the cheapest quote the best?

Not usually. A low price may leave out drying time or readings, and a job that is not dried properly costs more later.

Do I need a written contract?

Yes. Read the scope, the price and any assignment of insurance benefits before you sign.

How do I know the structure is actually dry?

Ask for daily moisture readings and a final reading that matches unaffected materials.
