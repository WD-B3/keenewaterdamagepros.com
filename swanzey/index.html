Five villages, one town: where Swanzey water damage starts

The town's own history page describes Swanzey as 45 square miles of land in the southwest corner of New Hampshire, split into five villages: Swanzey Center, North Swanzey, East Swanzey, West Swanzey and Westport. NH 10, NH 12 and NH 32 run through it. That spread matters, because a house on a village street, a farmhouse on a back road and a ranch near the river face different water problems.

Four historic covered bridges cross the Ashuelot River and its South Branch here, among them the Slate, Cresson, West Swanzey and Carlton bridges. Homes along a river valley are the ones that should look up the FEMA flood map for their exact address, and ask their insurer what a standard policy leaves out.

When water is spreading in Swanzey

Most calls we get from this side of the county start the same way. A supply line lets go while someone is at work, a water heater tank weeps into a finished basement, or a thaw sends meltwater against a foundation wall. If any of that is happening to you, call the 24/7 line before you start mopping, because the first hours decide whether the subfloor and wall bottoms can be dried in place.

Our crews arrive with truck-mounted and portable extraction units, air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, moisture meters and a thermal camera. We map the wet area first, then pull water, then dry. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Swanzey, including nights and holidays. No arrival time is promised, but you will hear plainly what we can do on the first visit.

Flood and insurance

A burst supply line and a rising river are different events to an insurer. Pipe failures are often covered, and river water usually needs its own policy. Ask your agent which applies to you. Coverage is their call, and our readings and photos are there to help.
