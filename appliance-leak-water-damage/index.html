Appliance leak emergency: stop the water at the valve

Most appliance floods come from a supply hose, a fill valve or a drain line, and most can be stopped at the shutoff valve behind or below the machine. Close it, then unplug the appliance if the floor around the outlet is dry. If you have to step in water to reach the plug, leave it and shut off the breaker for that room only if the panel is dry.

Then call our 24/7 line. Tell us which appliance failed, how long it ran and which floor it is on. A washer in a second-floor laundry room needs a different plan than a refrigerator in a ground-floor kitchen, because water from upstairs reaches the ceiling and walls below.

Why an appliance leak is bigger than it looks

A leak behind a washing machine can run for hours unseen. Water spreads under flooring, soaks the subfloor and travels along joists. A dishwasher that leaks slowly can soak the cabinet base and the subfloor under it for weeks before a stain shows. Refrigerator line leaks are among the sneakiest because the water runs behind the unit and under the floor.

That is why moisture mapping matters here. We check the floor, the baseboards, the wall behind the appliance and the ceiling below with a meter and a thermal camera, then dry what is wet. If the water reached a kitchen cabinet base, our kitchen water damage work covers the cabinets and toe kicks.

How we dry out after an appliance failure

We extract standing water, pull baseboards where water is trapped behind them and set air movers and a dehumidifier. Hardwood and laminate often need special drying mats or removal if they have started to cup, and carpet pad usually comes out. If the failure was on an upper floor, we also check the ceiling and walls below. Our pages on hardwood floor water damage and ceiling water damage repair cover those materials in detail.

A leak that sat for weeks may involve mold in the cabinet base or behind the wall. We tell you what we see rather than guess, and we point you to our mold remediation page if testing or containment is needed.

What affects the cost

Appliance leaks range from one wet floor to several wet rooms, so the scope sets the price.

  • How long it ran. A slow weekend leak wets more than a hose caught in minutes
  • Flooring type. Hardwood, laminate and carpet each dry or fail differently
  • Rooms affected. A second-floor leak adds the ceiling and wall below
  • Cabinet and trim work. Removal and drying of toe kicks and cabinet bases add labor
  • Drying days. Toe kicks and subfloor dry slowly, and the readings decide the length

Insurance and appliance leak water damage

Water damage from a sudden appliance failure is often treated differently from long-term seepage, and the appliance itself is usually not part of the water damage coverage. Policies vary, so read yours or ask your insurer. Our readings and photos back up your account of what happened.

Appliance Leak Water Damage questions, answered

Is there a cutoff time for appliance leak calls?

There is no cutoff. Close the shutoff valve or unplug the unit if the floor is dry, then call at any hour. Crews are dispatched the same day when possible, with no arrival time promised.

Is it safe to unplug a leaking washing machine?

Only if the floor around the outlet is dry. If you must step in water to reach the plug, leave it, shut off the breaker for that room only if the panel is dry, and call.

Do I need drying if the floor looks dry now?

Maybe. Water can sit under flooring and behind baseboards for days. A moisture meter tells us in minutes whether the structure is still wet.

Should I replace the hose or the whole appliance?

That is a plumber or appliance technician question. Braided hoses fail at the fittings, so ask them to check the connections. We focus on the water and the drying.
