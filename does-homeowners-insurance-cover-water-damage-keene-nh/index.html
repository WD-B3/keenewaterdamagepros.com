Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Keene, NH?

Sometimes, and it depends on how the water got in. Policies usually treat a sudden pipe failure very differently from a flood or a slow leak, and the difference decides if you are paying a claim or a repair bill.

The short version: sudden is covered more often than gradual

Most standard homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental. A supply line that bursts, a water heater that fails or an ice dam that pushes water under shingles in one storm usually fall in that group. Water that seeped for months from a dripping trap or a failing seal is often treated as maintenance, and many policies exclude it.

Policy wording varies by company and by endorsement, so treat this as a map and not an answer. Your declarations page and the policy itself are the documents that count. If you cannot find them, your agent can send them.

What is commonly covered and what is commonly excluded

SituationHow policies often treat it
Burst or frozen pipe, sudden failureOften covered, including damage to walls and floors
Appliance hose or supply line failureOften covered, though the failed part itself usually is not
Roof leak from a stormOften covered if the roof was in reasonable condition
Slow leak over weeks or monthsOften excluded as wear, neglect or maintenance
River flooding or surface waterUsually excluded, needs a separate flood policy
Sewer or drain backupUsually excluded unless you bought an endorsement

Flood is the biggest gap

People often assume that a flooded basement is a water damage claim. If the water came in from outside, through the walls, floor or a window well after heavy rain or snowmelt, it is generally flood or groundwater, and a standard policy excludes it. A separate flood policy covers it, and those can have a waiting period before they start.

That is why it helps to read your policy before the storm, not after. If you live near the Ashuelot River or any brook, check the FEMA flood map for your exact address and ask your agent what you have.

What New Hampshire's insurance regulator says

The New Hampshire Insurance Department states that homeowners policies do not cover most flooding and that a separate flood policy is needed. It adds that there may be a 30-day delay before a new flood policy covers a loss.

The department's homeowner FAQ also says most policies exclude water that backs up through sewers or drains, and that many companies offer that coverage as an endorsement for an additional premium. If you have a sump pump or a floor drain in the basement, that endorsement is worth asking about.

What to do in the first hours so a claim is easier

Stop the source if you can. Take photos and video of the damage before you move anything. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them unless they are unsafe. Call your insurer or agent to report the loss, and ask what they want you to do about emergency mitigation, because most policies expect you to prevent further damage.

Then call a restoration company to extract water and start drying. Drying quickly is both a policy duty and the best way to limit the final cost. We log moisture readings daily and photograph the work, so there is a record of what was wet and what was done. See our water extraction page for the process.

Questions to ask your insurer

  • Does my policy cover sudden pipe failures and appliance leaks, and what is excluded?
  • Do I have sewer or drain backup coverage, and what is the limit?
  • What is my deductible for water damage, and is it a flat amount or a percentage?
  • Does the policy cover the cost of tearing out and replacing walls to reach a leak?
  • What does the policy require me to do to prevent further damage, and what proof do you need?

Write down the answers and who gave them. If something is unclear, ask for it in writing. Burst pipe and flood claims in Keene often involve basements and finished lower levels, so it also helps to know how your policy treats finished space. Our burst pipe page and basement page describe what we typically find.

A note for homes outside Keene

The same questions apply in the surrounding towns. A farmhouse in Westmoreland on a private well and septic system and a house on public water in Marlborough may face different failures but the same coverage rules. Ask your agent which causes of loss your policy names.

More on does insurance cover water damage?

Will my insurance pay for a burst pipe in Keene?

Often, if the break was sudden and accidental, but it depends on your policy and any exclusions. Ask your insurer, and document the damage before you clean up.

Does homeowners insurance cover a flooded basement from groundwater?

Usually not. The New Hampshire Insurance Department says homeowners policies do not cover most flooding, and a separate flood policy is needed.

Do I have to hire the restoration company my insurer suggests?

Policies generally let you choose your own contractor, but check yours and ask your insurer what they require. We do not promise coverage or approval.

How soon should I report water damage?

As soon as you can. Many policies require prompt notice, and waiting can make it harder to show what was sudden and what was gradual.

Does homeowners insurance cover mold after water damage?

Policies handle mold in different ways. Some limit it, some exclude it and some cover it only after a covered water loss, so ask your insurer what yours says.
